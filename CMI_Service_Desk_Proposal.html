1 · Summary

What was asked, and what we propose

The request is to see CMI as a proper IT service desk: three clearly separated sections instead of a list of form links, a flow like the reference diagram, and approval from the requester's Head of Department (HOD) by email.

The request

  • The home page should show big sections: end users see IT Requests and IT Incidents; core users (the IT team) also see Change Management.
  • Each section has its own categories (for example Access Request, Network Issue, Emergency Change) and its own steps from start to close.
  • An incident can lead to a change when a permanent fix is needed.
  • An admin keeps the master settings: categories, priority, SLA, workflows, approvers, support teams, statuses.
  • A requester types their HOD's email. The HOD gets a link and approves or rejects from the email.

The short answer: most of this is configuration

CMI is already a configurable system. Every category becomes a form, and every approval chain is a workflow, set up by an admin on screen with no programming. Existing parts cover about two thirds of what is needed. Four additions complete it:

NEW

Three-section home page

Each form is placed in a section and shown under a large tile.
NEW

HOD approval by email link

The HOD approves with one click. No account or password is needed.
NEW

Support groups and SLA rules

Assign work to a team, with response and resolution targets for each type.
NEW

"Change required?" bridge

Raise a linked change request straight from an incident.

Changes to servers, network, applications or configuration are planned, risk-assessed and approved before anyone touches production. The type of change decides how many approvals it needs. This section is visible only to core users (the IT team); end users do not see it on their home page.

8 · HOD approval by email

The requester names their HOD, and the HOD approves from email

The HOD does not need a CMI account or password. The email itself carries a secure, single-use approval link that is tied to that one ticket and that one email address.

1

Requester submits
and types the HOD's email in the form
2

CMI emails the HOD
a summary of the request and an approval link
3

HOD opens the link
sees the full request, then Approve, Reject or Send back, with a comment
4

Workflow continues
to the next approver or to the IT team
5

Requester is notified
and the decision is saved in the ticket history

How the admin sets it up

  • In the form, add a field of the new type "Approver email", labelled "HOD email".
  • In the workflow, add a stage "HOD approval", approver: "email from field: HOD email".
  • The stage can be first, in the middle or conditional, like any other stage.
  • Any form in any section can use it. It is not limited to IT Requests.

Safeguards

  • Only company email domains are accepted. CMI already has this setting.
  • The requester cannot enter their own email as HOD.
  • The link works once, for that ticket only, and expires (suggested: 7 days).
  • If the request is edited after approval, CMI asks the HOD again, as it already does for approvals today.

Convenience

  • Reminder email if there is no decision after 2 days. The IT team can also resend it.
  • CMI remembers the last HOD each user entered and pre-fills it next time.
  • If the HOD is also a CMI user, the request appears in their "Pending My Approval" list too.
  • Audit: who approved, when, from which link, with what comment.

10 · SLA rules

SLA rules for each type

Each section measures something different. Incidents are about restoring service fast, requests are about delivering on time, and changes are about deciding and implementing safely. The admin can change every target below; they are suggested starting values.

IT Requests

Targets by category. The clock starts after HOD approval.

CategoryRespondFulfil
Access request4 h1 day
Software installation4 h2 days
Hardware request4 h5 days*
IT services4 h2 days
Other request1 day3 days

* If hardware must be purchased, the clock pauses while it is "Waiting for procurement".

IT Incidents

Targets by priority. The clock starts when the incident is reported.

PriorityRespondResolveHours
Critical15 min4 h24×7
High1 h8 hOffice
Medium4 h2 daysOffice
Low1 day5 daysOffice

"Respond" means a support person has picked up the ticket and contacted the user.

Change Management

Targets by change type: how fast it is decided and closed.

TypeDecisionClose after implementing
StandardNone (pre-approved)Done within 2 days
NormalCAB within 5 daysReview within 2 days
EmergencyECAB within 2 h, 24×7Review within 1 day

Changes are also checked against their planned window: running past the planned end time is flagged.

How the SLA clock works (same for all three)

Office hours

Counted only during office hours (suggested Mon–Sat, 9:30–18:30, excluding holidays), except rows marked 24×7. "Day" means one working day.

Clock pauses

While waiting on someone outside IT: HOD approval, "Returned" to the requester for more information, or waiting for procurement.

Warning at 75%

Email to the assignee and the support group lead. The ticket turns amber in lists and on the home page.

Breach at 100%

Email to the IT Head. The ticket turns red and counts as "breached" in the SLA report for each section, group and person.

12 · Rollout

Delivered in three steps, each usable on its own

Each phase ends with something management can see and use. Timelines will be confirmed once the decisions on the next page are made.

Phase 1

The three desks

  • Sections master and the tiled home page: end users see 2 sections, core users see 3
  • All 15 category forms set up (5 per section) with their questions
  • Workflows for each, including Standard / Normal / Emergency change rules
  • Closing labels: Fulfilled, Resolved, Implemented
  • Per-section counts on the home page and in reports
Shows the customizable nature: almost all of this phase is configuration.
Phase 2

HOD approval and teams

  • "Approver email" field and "HOD from form" workflow stage
  • Secure single-use email link and the approval page
  • Reminders, resend, and remembering the last HOD
  • Support groups and teams; "assign to group"; group work queues
Phase 3

Service levels and linking

  • SLA rules for each type, 75% warnings, breach escalation and an SLA report
  • Incident priority from "who is affected / can work continue"
  • "Raise Change" from an incident (pre-filled and linked)
  • Change calendar
Later (optional)

Extras

  • Knowledge base / FAQs
  • Problem management as a 4th section
  • Asset register / CMDB
  • Creating tickets from incoming email
